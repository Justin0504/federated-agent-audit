## Abstract
Agent delegation can disclose sensitive information across organizational boundaries. We introduce a2a.privacy/v1, an experimental A2A metadata extension, and an auditor that checks disclosure, purpose, forwarding and cross-message inference from local labels. The center receives no raw message text, but metadata leakage, incorrect labels and missing observations remain possible. We separately evaluate release checks that bind authorization, case relevance and factual support. On 20 new authored tasks, a frozen structured gate preserves all eight benign completions, reduces literal forbidden-marker release from two tasks to zero and unsupported-value release from four to zero, and returns unknown on nine tasks. On 1,132 archived AgentDojo trajectories, destination binding reduces native exfiltration success from 165/808 to 0/808 in action-prefix replay; clean completion falls from 198/324 to 179/324. Replay stops at the first block and does not measure recovery. A separate live 14B comparison runs eight methods on 32 development-disjoint tasks. Ungated execution completes 10/32 tasks with one native attack success in 16 attacked tasks; defended variants complete 0--12 tasks with zero observed attacks. These small counts do not establish privacy or utility superiority. We release code and artifacts for jointly assessing attack outcomes, task completion and cost under explicit trust assumptions.

## Evidence limits retained in this version
- The new 20-task set was authored after gate freezing, but is not externally
  authored or human-adjudicated. Slot completion and literal privacy-marker
  matching do not establish general semantic utility or confidentiality.
- External AgentDojo results use 1,132 archived action-prefix replays and native
  predicates; they are not new live model runs. Stopping on first block exposes
  utility loss. Historical-label mismatches and checker exceptions are reported.
- The separate live formal comparison contains 256 episodes on 32 task IDs and
  eight methods, one 14B model and one seed. Native attack successes are sparse;
  zero of 16 does not establish privacy superiority.
- All five release-gate arms reuse the same generated proposal. Partial arms are
  ablations, not reproductions of other papers' systems.
